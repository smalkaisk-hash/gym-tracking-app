This policy explains how we handle **consumer health data** as defined by the Washington
My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373). It applies to Washington residents and to anyone
whose consumer health data is collected in Washington. Our general practices are in the
[Privacy Policy](/privacy.html); where the two differ for consumer health data, this
document governs.

## 1. Categories of consumer health data we collect, and why

We collect only what the app needs to show you your own training history. Exhaustively:

| Category of consumer health data | What it is | Why we collect it and how it is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bodily measurements** | Your body weight entries and the date of each | To display your weight history and chart, and to show body weight alongside the session it was recorded in. Used for nothing else |
| **Fitness and exercise data** | Workout type, date, duration, per-exercise sets, repetitions and weights lifted, and any notes you type | To store and display your workout history, per-exercise progress charts, streaks, weekly-goal progress and muscle-group breakdowns — all shown only to you |
| **Health-related demographic data** | Your gender, if you choose to provide it (**optional** — the app works without it) | We store it as part of your profile and show it back to you in Settings. It does not currently affect any calculation, chart or recommendation in the app, and it is not used for anything else |

**We do not collect** any other health data. Specifically we do not collect or infer:
diagnoses, conditions, symptoms, medications, treatments, biometric or genetic data,
reproductive or sexual health information, mental health information, precise location,
or health data purchased or received from any other company.

## 2. Sources of consumer health data

**You are the only source.** Every item above is data you typed into the app yourself. We
do not obtain consumer health data from data brokers, advertising networks, public
records, health platforms (Apple Health, Google Fit, HealthKit), wearables, affiliates,
or any other third party. We do not derive or infer health data from your behaviour,
location, or purchases.

## 4. We do not sell consumer health data

**We have never sold consumer health data and we will not.** Selling consumer health data
without a signed, statutorily-specified authorization is unlawful under RCW 19.373.050,
and we do not seek such authorizations because we do not sell data.

We also do not use consumer health data for advertising, marketing, targeted
advertising, profiling, or any form of data mining. FitPulse contains no advertising or
analytics code of any kind.

**Nevada residents:** we likewise do not sell your consumer health data as defined by
Nevada SB 370, and the same statements above apply to you.

## 5. Consent

We collect consumer health data on one of two bases, and never on any other:

- **To provide the service you requested.** When you log a workout or a weigh-in, we
  store and display it because that is the product you asked us for — this is the
  "necessary to provide a product or service the consumer has requested" basis in
  RCW 19.373.030.
- **With your consent.** Where anything falls outside that, we ask first, separately, in
  a way you have to affirmatively act on.

**We also ask for your explicit consent up front, and we ask everyone.** At sign-up there
is a second, separate tick-box — not the one that accepts the Terms — covering the storage
of your body weight, your training history and your gender. We do that because the GDPR,
which applies to users in the EEA and UK, treats this data as a special category that
consent alone can unlock (Art. 9(2)(a)); the "requested service" basis above is enough for
this Act but not for that one. Rather than run two different regimes, we take the stricter
path for every user. So for a Washington resident both bases are satisfied at once, and
the [Privacy Policy §4](/privacy.html) describes the same processing from the GDPR side.
The two documents are not in conflict — they are the same practice under two statutes.

Our consent requests disclose, before you decide: the categories of consumer health data
involved, the purpose and the specific ways the data will be used, the categories of
entities it would be shared with, and how to withdraw consent.

We never obtain consent through general terms of use, through a pre-ticked box, through
inaction, or through interface design that pushes you toward accepting. Consent to
collection is always separate and distinct from consent to sharing.

**To withdraw consent**, email **smalkaisk@gmail.com** or delete your account in
Settings → Delete account, which removes all consumer health data immediately.

**If we ever want to collect a new category of consumer health data, or use an existing
category for a new purpose, or share it with anyone,** we will disclose that here and
obtain your affirmative consent *before* doing so. We will not apply a new purpose to
data already collected without asking you.

## 6. Geofencing

We do not, and will not, operate a geofence around any facility that provides in-person
health care services. FitPulse does not request or collect location data at all.

## 7. Your rights, and how to exercise them

Under RCW 19.373.040 you have the right to:

1. **Confirm** whether we are collecting, sharing or selling your consumer health data,
   and to **access** it — including a list of all third parties with whom we have shared
   it (which, per §3, is none).
2. **Withdraw consent** to our collection and sharing of your consumer health data.
3. **Delete** your consumer health data. RCW 19.373.040(3) extends this to archived and
   backup systems, and we can satisfy it completely, because **we keep no backups**:
   - **Live systems: immediately.** Deleting your account removes every workout,
     weigh-in, preference and the account itself at once, and we instruct our processors
     to do the same.
   - **Archived and backup systems: there are none.** Our database plan includes no
     automated backups, and we do not take manual ones. There is no archive, no
     point-in-time snapshot and no exported copy of the database anywhere in our network,
     so a deletion leaves nothing behind to age out.

   The honest corollary, stated here rather than buried in the Terms: because no backup
   exists, a deletion cannot be reversed by us, and data lost through a provider failure
   cannot be recovered either. Settings → **Export backup** exists so that the copy you
   control is the one that survives.

**How to exercise them:**

- **Immediately, yourself:** Settings → **Delete account** deletes your account and every
  workout, weigh-in and preference in one irreversible action. Settings → **Export
  backup** gives you a complete copy of your data as a JSON file.
- **By email:** **smalkaisk@gmail.com**. We will not require you to create an
  account to make a request.

**Our timelines:** we respond without undue delay and in all cases **within 45 days** of
receipt. We may extend once by a further 45 days where reasonably necessary, and we will
tell you within the first 45 days if we do, with the reason.

**Appeals:** if we decline a request, we will tell you why and how to appeal. Send an
appeal to **smalkaisk@gmail.com** with "MHMDA appeal" in the subject. We respond
in writing within 45 days, explaining the reasons for the decision. If we deny the
appeal, we will give you a link to submit a complaint to the **Washington State Attorney
General** at https://www.atg.wa.gov/file-complaint.

## 8. How we protect consumer health data

- Every database table enforces **row-level security** keyed to your account ID, so your
  rows are readable only by your own authenticated session — enforced by the database
  itself, not by application code.
- All transmission is over **HTTPS/TLS**; data is **encrypted at rest** by our database
  provider.
- Access to production data is restricted to **one person — the sole operator of the
  service** — through the database provider's administrative console. No employees,
  contractors or other persons have access, because there are none. One person is the
  minimum necessary to operate the service, which is the standard RCW 19.373.060 sets.
- **The app itself holds no credential that could reach anyone else's rows.** The key
  shipped in the browser is the provider's public key, and every read and write made with
  it is constrained by the row-level security above. The one key that can bypass those
  rules is never in the app, never in the published files, and is used only by an offline
  script on the operator's own machine.
- We collect only the data the app needs to function, and we do not retain consumer
  health data after you delete it, on the terms set out in §7.3 above: gone from live
  systems at once, with no backup or archive for it to persist in.
