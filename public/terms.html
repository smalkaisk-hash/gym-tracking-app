11.8 Working offline, and what happens when two devices disagree. FitPulse keeps a copy of your data on each device you use it on, so it works without a connection. You should know how those copies are reconciled, because it decides what survives:

  (a) a change you make while offline is held on that device and marked as not yet synced, and is sent the next time the app successfully writes to, or loads from, our servers. There is no background retry — the app is not running when it is closed, so a change made offline waits until you open FitPulse again on that device;

  (b) when the app loads your data, a not-yet-synced change on that device takes precedence over our server's copy of the same record, and a not-yet-synced record that never reached us is kept rather than dropped;

  (c) between devices, the later change to reach our servers is the one that survives. There is no merge of two edits to the same session and no version history: if you edit the same session on two devices, the second write replaces the first, and the first is gone;

  (d) deletion is not held offline. If a deletion cannot reach our servers the app puts the record back and tells you it did not delete — try again when you are connected. A deletion that did reach us stays done, and a later load does not resurrect it;

  (e) a change that has not reached our servers exists only on that device, so clause 11.3 applies to it: clearing your browser storage, uninstalling, or losing the device loses it.

This is a deliberate design for a single-user training log, not a defect, and clause 14.7 allocates the risk accordingly. The practical advice: make large edits on one device while you are online, and open the app on any device that has been used offline before you rely on what another device shows.
