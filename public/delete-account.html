Delete your FitPulse account

You can delete your FitPulse account and everything stored in it at any time. You do not need to give a reason, and you do not need to contact us first.

There are two routes. Both delete the same things.

From inside the app

  1. Open FitPulse and sign in.
  2. Go to Settings (the gear icon in the header).
  3. Scroll to Account and choose Delete account.
  4. Confirm. You will be asked twice, because this cannot be undone.

Without the app

If you can't reach the app — you've uninstalled it, you're locked out, or you're on a device you don't want to install it on — email smalkaisk@gmail.com from the address your account uses, with the subject Delete my account.

We will delete the account within 30 days and confirm by email when it is done. We may ask you to confirm from the account's email address if the request arrives from a different one; that check exists to stop somebody else deleting your data.

What gets deleted

Deleting your account permanently removes:

  • your login and email address;
  • every workout you have logged, including exercises, sets, notes and durations;
  • your entire body-weight log;
  • your settings and preferences, including your name, gender, units, weekly goal and chosen day names;
  • your record of consent to health-data processing.

Deletion is immediate and permanent once confirmed in the app. There is no grace period, no archive and no recycle bin, and we cannot restore an account after the fact — not even if you ask us the same day.

The copy of your data cached on the device you deleted from is cleared at the same time.

Other devices you were signed in on are a manual step. The app keeps an offline copy in each browser you used it in, and we cannot reach into those browsers to erase it — a device that is offline, or that you never open again, keeps its copy until you remove it. On any such device, open your browser's settings and clear site data for FitPulse. Signing out of the app clears it too, if you can still reach a signed-in session there.

We would rather tell you this than imply a remote wipe we cannot perform.

What does not get deleted

  • Backups you exported yourself. A JSON file you downloaded from Settings → Data → Export backup lives on your own device and is yours to keep or delete.
  • Infrastructure logs. Our providers keep short-lived technical records of requests — the time, the network address a request came from, and what was asked for. They are not your training data, they are deleted automatically on the schedule in our Privacy Policy, and we do not consult them about a deleted account.
  • Email between us — what you have sent, our replies, and any notice we have sent you. If you have written to us, that thread stays in our mailbox for the period given in our Privacy Policy — it is not deleted by deleting your account. The same is true of a notice we sent you, such as a dormant-account warning or a policy-change notice: it is a message addressed to you and our record that we gave the notice. We use both only to handle your request, to send the notice, and to show that we did. Ask us and we will delete the thread too.
  • Our authentication provider's security log. Signing in, signing up and resetting a password are recorded by the provider that performs them, with the time, the network address and the email address used. It is a security record rather than your training data, it is never used to reconstruct a deleted account, and the Privacy Policy gives the period it is kept for.
  • Records we are legally required to retain. Where the law requires us to keep something, we keep only that, only for as long as required, and we do not use it for anything else.

Server backups are not on that list, because we do not have any. Our database plan includes no automated backups and we do not take manual ones, so there is no archive, no point-in-time snapshot and no second copy anywhere in our systems for a deleted account to survive in.

That cuts both ways, and we would rather say so plainly: because no backup exists, we cannot reverse a deletion for you — not the same day, not ever — and data lost through a provider failure cannot be recovered either. Export first.

Washington State residents: the My Health My Data Act gives you the right to have consumer health data deleted from all parts of our network, including archived and backup systems. We can satisfy that completely, for the reason above: there are no archived or backup systems for it to reach. See our Consumer Health Data Privacy notice for the full statement of your rights and how to appeal if you think we have got this wrong.

Export first if you want to keep anything

Deletion does not export. If you want your training history, take a copy before you delete: Settings → Data → Export backup writes a JSON file containing your workouts, your body-weight log, your settings and your account details, including the record of the consent you gave.
