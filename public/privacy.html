FitPulse Privacy Policy

Version 1.0 · Effective 1 August 2026 · Last reviewed 1 August 2026

The short version

FitPulse is a personal gym-tracking app. Here is everything that matters, in one place:

  • What we hold: your email address, the name you chose, your optional gender, your unit and display preferences, and the workouts and body weights you log.
  • Why: to run the app for you. That's the whole reason. There is no second purpose.
  • Your training and body-weight data is health data. We treat it as such, and we only process it because you gave us explicit consent when you created your account.
  • We do not sell it, share it, advertise against it, or analyse it. There are no ad networks, no analytics, no trackers, and no profiling in this app.
  • Only you can read your rows. Every database table is locked to your account ID by a database-level rule, not by application code.
  • You can delete everything, permanently, yourself, from Settings → Delete account. You can export a full copy from Settings → Export backup.
  • Who else touches it: the small, named list of infrastructure companies in §6. No one else. Ever.
  • Where you live changes your rights, not our practices. We do the same thing with everyone's data wherever they are — one database, one set of providers, no ads, no selling — but the law gives different people different rights over it. §9 has a section for the EEA and UK, for California, for Washington State and for the rest of the US. Washington residents also have a separate Consumer Health Data Privacy notice, which that state requires to be its own document.
  • Questions or requests: smalkaisk@gmail.com.

The rest of this document is the detail behind those statements.

3. Health data — the important part

Your body weight, your gender, and your training history are health-related personal data. Under the GDPR this is "special category" data (Art. 9); under Washington's My Health My Data Act it is "consumer health data"; under the FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule it is "PHR identifiable health information".

We process it on the basis of your explicit consent (GDPR Art. 9(2)(a)), given by the separate, unticked checkbox shown at sign-up. That consent:

  • is asked for separately from accepting the Terms — it is never bundled;
  • covers only the purpose in §4, and nothing else;
  • can be withdrawn at any time, by deleting your account, or by deleting all your data from Settings if you want to keep the login;
  • when withdrawn, does not make the processing before withdrawal unlawful.

Being straight about what withdrawal means here. This data is not a side-effect of the app; it is the app. There is no version of FitPulse that keeps showing you your training history while no longer holding it. So withdrawing consent and deleting the data are the same action, and we have not invented a middle setting that would pretend otherwise. Both routes are one tap from Settings, neither asks you for a reason, and neither costs you anything — which is the standard the GDPR sets for withdrawal being as easy as consenting (Art. 7(3)). If you would rather we deleted it for you, email us and we will.

Washington residents: see the separate Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy, which the My Health My Data Act requires us to publish and link prominently.

To remove the device copy and nothing else, sign out. Signing out clears every key in the table above from that browser and leaves your account and its data on the server untouched, so it is the right control on a shared or borrowed computer. Your browser's own "clear site data" control does the same thing.

Settings → Delete all my data is a different and much larger action: it erases your workouts, weigh-ins and settings on the server as well, for every device, and it cannot be undone. We have named the two differently on purpose — an earlier version of this policy described the second one as a way to clear the device copy, which is exactly the mistake that would cost somebody their training history.

8. Security

Concrete measures, not adjectives:

  • All traffic between your device and our servers uses HTTPS/TLS.
  • Every database table enforces row-level security: a policy in the database itself restricts every read and write to rows whose owner matches the signed-in account ID. This is enforced by the database, not by app code, so an application bug cannot expose another user's rows.
  • Passwords are stored only as salted hashes by our authentication provider. We cannot see your password.
  • Changing your password requires your current password.
  • Data is encrypted at rest by our database provider.
  • Access to production data is restricted to one person — the sole operator of the service — through the database provider's administrative console. No employees, contractors or other persons have access, because there are none.
  • The app itself holds no credential that could reach anyone else's rows. The key shipped in the browser is the provider's public key, and every read and write made with it is constrained by the row-level security above. The one key that can bypass those rules is never in the app, never in the published files, and is used only by an offline script on the operator's own machine. (This is the same statement as Consumer Health Data Privacy §8 — one fact, worded once.)

No system is perfectly secure, and we don't claim otherwise. We do not use the phrases "military-grade", "industry-standard" or "bank-level" here on purpose: they mean nothing and regulators treat them as deceptive.

If a breach happens, we will:

  • notify the relevant supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware, where the breach is likely to result in a risk to you (GDPR Art. 33);
  • notify you directly, without undue delay, where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to your rights (GDPR Art. 34);
  • notify you and the FTC within 60 calendar days, and prominent media if 500+ people in one jurisdiction are affected, as the FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule requires of health apps that are not covered by HIPAA.

10. International transfers

Your account, workouts, weigh-ins and preferences are stored and processed in Ireland, in our database provider's eu-west-1 region. Ireland is in the European Economic Area, so this data is not transferred outside the EEA, and no separate transfer mechanism is needed for it.

Two narrower points, stated rather than glossed over:

  • Password-reset and confirmation email is sent by our database provider's built-in email service. It carries your email address and the message text — never your workouts or weights. Our provider does not publish the region its shared email infrastructure runs in, and it is a United States company, so we treat this as a transfer outside the EEA. It is covered by the Standard Contractual Clauses approved by the European Commission, which form part of our provider's data processing agreement. We will send you a copy on request. This concerns your email address only — never your health data, which stays in Ireland as described above.

11. Children

FitPulse is not intended for anyone under 18, and the Terms of Service set the same floor (§4.1) — creating an account requires confirming you are 18 or older. We do not knowingly collect data from children. If you believe a child has created an account, email smalkaisk@gmail.com and we will delete it and its data promptly. We do not serve ads and have no third-party analytics, so nothing about a child's data would be transmitted anywhere even in that case.

12. Changes to this policy

We keep a version number and effective date at the top, and every previous version is kept in the project's Git history. Email us and we will send you any earlier version.

  • Minor corrections (typos, clearer wording, a new address) take effect on posting.
  • Material changes — a new purpose, a new recipient, a new data type, a longer retention period — will be announced in advance by in-app notice and by email to your registered address, with a plain summary of what changed, before they take effect.
  • Where a change affects your health data — a new category, a new purpose, or a new recipient — we will obtain your affirmative consent first, as the My Health My Data Act requires. We will not apply a change to health data collected under an earlier version of this policy without asking you.
  • We review this policy at least once every 12 months even if nothing has changed, and update the "last reviewed" date above.

We will never make a material change quietly and rely on your continued use as consent.
